HaLow Link History

RSSI (solid) is received signal strength - closer to 0 is stronger. Noise (dashed) is the background floor at that end. What actually matters is the gap between them (signal-to-noise ratio) - a bigger gap means more margin before the link starts degrading. A wide gap now is expected on a bench; expect it to shrink once deployed at real range.
Fraction of frames that needed at least one retransmission. Occasional small spikes are normal (interference blips). A sustained climb is the real warning sign - it's literally what the optimizer's degradation detector watches for (currently: sustained average above 15%).
Current HaLow channel width in MHz. This is a step chart - it only moves when a config change actually happens, so a flat line just means nothing's been changed yet (expected until the rule-based optimizer is built and starts issuing commands).
Actual data throughput (solid) against the currently negotiated PHY rate (dashed, the "ceiling"). This ratio is what the optimizer's bandwidth widen/narrow decision watches - sustained high utilization triggers widening, sustained low utilization (over a much longer window) triggers narrowing.

2.4GHz Link History (STA → downstream clients)

Which channel the STA's 2.4GHz radio is broadcasting on for downstream clients to join. Step chart, same reasoning as HaLow bandwidth above - flat means unchanged.
Per-client signal strength as seen by the STA, one line per connected device on the downstream 2.4GHz network. A client with no line for a period was disconnected during that window.
Per-client retry fraction, same interpretation as HaLow retries above - occasional small spikes are normal, a sustained climb on one client is the real signal to investigate.
